# Cover Letter Drafting Prompt

Copy-paste this at the start of a Claude conversation when you want a cover letter drafted for a specific role. Attach four things:

1. The **cover letter style guide** (`cover-letter-style-guide.md`)
2. The **master resume** (`resume.md`) — for context on experience
3. The **story bank** (`style-guides/story-bank.md` — the document with headline stories, security stories, helpdesk stories, networking stories)
4. The **job description**

---

## The prompt

I'd like a cover letter drafted for the attached role. I've given you four things: my cover letter style guide (follow it), my master resume (for context), my story bank (pull from this), and the job description (the role I'm applying for).

Before drafting, do this:

1. Read the JD and tell me: what is the **one central concern** of this role? What is the hiring manager most hoping to find in a candidate? State this in one or two sentences. Don't hedge.
    
2. From the story bank, identify the **2–3 stories** that best match that central concern. Briefly explain why each one fits and which you'd pick as the load-bearing story for paragraph 2. Recommend one.
    
3. Flag anything uncertain — places where the JD asks for something I haven't obviously done, where a story might need careful framing, or where the company context suggests a specific tone.
    
4. Wait for my sign-off on story selection before drafting.
    

Once I've signed off:

5. Draft the cover letter, 3–4 short paragraphs, following the structure in the style guide. Be specific, confident, and story-forward. Do not paraphrase my resume. Do not mention gaps, weaknesses, or things I haven't done.
    
6. After the draft, tell me what story you used, what choices you made (tone, close, any weaving-in), and anything you're uncertain about.
    

---

## Notes to self

- Make sure Claude does steps 1–4 before drafting. If it skips the audit and starts writing, stop it and ask for the audit. The story selection is where most of the letter's quality is determined.
- If the first draft feels timid, say so. The calibration section of the style guide exists for a reason — don't accept a draft that undersells just because it's technically accurate.
- Keep cover letters short. If the draft is over 2/3 of a page, ask for a cut before sending.
- Don't let Claude invent details. If a story isn't in the story bank exactly as written, make sure the draft is faithful to the source.
