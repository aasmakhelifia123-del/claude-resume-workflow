# Story Bank

A library of **concrete situation → action → outcome** stories from your
career. Cover letters draw from here. Resumes occasionally do too, when a
bullet would land harder told as a story.

## What goes here

Stories with **specific moments and details** that stick — not generic
summaries. The reader of a cover letter should be able to picture the
moment.

Good story material:

- A migration you owned, with a specific decision or hiccup.
- An incident you handled, with what you noticed and what you changed.
- A platform you built, with the manual mess it replaced.
- A judgment call you made, with the constraint that forced it.
- A user-facing moment where you fixed something visible.

Each story should be **a paragraph at most**, framed as:

- **Situation** — the context, one line.
- **Action** — what *you* did. Not the team, not "we" — what *you* did.
- **Outcome** — what happened as a result. Concrete if possible.
- **When to use** — a hint to future-you (or to Claude) about what kinds
  of JDs this story is the right fit for.

## About me

A few sentences on who you are professionally — the *macro* version of
the through-line. Useful as cover-letter paragraph 1 framing.

> Write 3–5 sentences covering:
> - How long you've been in your field and where you started.
> - The arc of your career — what you've grown into, what kind of work
>   you keep gravitating toward.
> - What you're looking for next, in voice that's recognizably yours.

(Replace with your version.)

## Tips for filling this in

- **Drafting from scratch?** Open Claude Code in this repo and ask it to
  interview you. Give it `master.md` for context and say "interview me
  for stories — ask follow-up questions when an answer feels generic."
  Add stories as you go.
- **Tell each story to a friend out loud first.** The version that comes
  out of your mouth is almost always more vivid than the version you'd
  type. Write down that one.
- **Names and numbers earn their place.** "A user's iPad message
  vanished mid-board-meeting" works because *iPad* and *mid-board-meeting*
  are specific. "A high-pressure support situation" doesn't work.
- **Keep names out** when you don't want to commit them to a public-ish
  document — refer to "my last role" or "a regulated finance firm." But
  the story bank is local to your repo, so include whatever helps you
  retrieve the story later.
